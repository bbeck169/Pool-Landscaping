# Chapter 6 — Chemical Feed and Automation

*Original in-house authorship. External standards cited by name and link only.*

## Why automate

Manual sanitizer dosing works for a residential pool visited once a week, but it fails for pools with variable bather load, commercial demand, or absentee owners. Chemical automation delivers sanitizer and pH control continuously, in the small doses the pool actually needs, rather than in the large weekly slugs that manual service imposes. The result is more stable water, fewer eye complaints, less scale, and less chlorine consumed overall.

## Erosion feeders

Erosion feeders — sometimes called trichlor or brominators — hold slow-dissolving tablets in a pressure-regulated chamber. A small stream of pool return water is diverted through the chamber, dissolving a controlled amount of tablet, and the enriched water rejoins the main return line. The dose is set by a dial that limits the flow through the chamber. Erosion feeders are inexpensive, mechanical, and reliable, but they only work with tablet products and they slowly add CYA (with trichlor) or lower calcium hardness (with cal-hypo tablets, less common).

## Peristaltic and diaphragm pumps

Liquid sanitizer and muriatic acid are dispensed by small metering pumps mounted on the equipment pad. Peristaltic pumps use a rotating roller to squeeze a flexible tube, moving a precise, tube-defined volume per revolution. Diaphragm pumps use a reciprocating diaphragm and check valves to move liquid in metered strokes. Both types must be plumbed with an injection quill downstream of the heater, never before it — feeding acid into a hot heat exchanger will destroy it in weeks. All liquid feeders require a check valve to prevent siphon-back, a foot valve in the drum, and a properly-sized tie-in point.

## Salt chlorine generators

A salt chlorine generator (SCG) electrolyzes dissolved salt at 3,000–3,500 ppm across two titanium plates coated in a catalytic surface. Chlorine gas is produced at the anode and immediately reacts with water to form hypochlorous acid, which enters the return flow. The output is set as a percentage of maximum production. SCGs run only when the pump is running, so the pump schedule and the SCG capacity together define the daily chlorine budget for the pool. Cell cleaning with dilute muriatic acid every 3–6 months keeps calcium buildup from choking the plates; over-cleaning eats the coating and shortens cell life.

## ORP and pH controllers

Oxidation-reduction potential (ORP) is a millivolt reading that reflects the water's oxidative power — roughly, its ability to disinfect. A pH probe and an ORP probe wired to a controller can drive both the acid pump and the chlorine feeder in real time, holding both variables in a narrow band. Well-tuned ORP control produces water that is stable to within 0.1 pH and 50 mV, which no manual operator can match. Probes must be cleaned and calibrated on a regular schedule; a fouled probe reports false values and drives the automation toward chemistry that will not match a hand-test.

## Feed rate calculation

Every chemical automation setup needs a feed-rate check. For a liquid feeder, the operator times how many milliliters the pump delivers per minute at a fixed setting, multiplies by trade strength, and compares to the pool's chlorine demand. For an SCG, the label indicates pounds of chlorine per 24 hours of run time at 100 percent output — the operator computes what fraction of that is required and sets the percentage accordingly. Feed-rate audits catch failed check valves, empty drums, and mis-configured SCG cells before the pool loses residual.

## Interlocks and safety

Every liquid-chemical feed system must be interlocked with the circulation pump so that no chemical is dosed when the pump is off. In commercial installations, feeds are also interlocked with a flow switch that verifies actual water movement — the pump can be running with a closed valve, and dosing into no-flow return pipe is dangerous. Chlorine and acid drums must be stored in separated containment tubs so that a spill cannot mix the two.

## Automation controller platforms

Whole-pool controllers integrate the pump schedule, the heater, the SCG or feeders, the lights, the water features, and the chemistry probes into one dashboard, often with a phone app. When installing or servicing an automation platform, keep a wiring diagram at the equipment pad and always update the manufacturer's firmware to the current version — old firmware has known bugs that can freeze the heater in an on state or run the pump dry.

## Field practice

At every visit, verify chemical drum levels, tubing condition, and the presence of both check valves and foot valves. Time-check the feed rate at least monthly. Clean and recalibrate ORP/pH probes on the manufacturer's schedule, not "when they look dirty." Never fill an empty acid drum without first neutralizing residue.
