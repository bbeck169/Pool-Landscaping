# Module 3 — Small Engine Repair · Chapter 2: The three-condition rule

*Original in-house authorship. Consult the OEM manual for model-specific fuel/spark specs.*

An engine that won't start has failed one of exactly three conditions:

1. **Compression** — the piston seals well enough to create pressure in the cylinder on the compression stroke.
2. **Spark** — the ignition system fires the plug at the right moment.
3. **Fuel** — clean fuel of the right mixture reaches the combustion chamber.

Diagnose in that order. Compression first because it's a hard mechanical fact — an engine with 30 psi compression will never start no matter how good the spark or fuel. Then spark (fast test with a spark tester). Then fuel (last because it's the messiest to inspect).

## Compression test

- Warm the engine (if it will run at all, run it 60 seconds).
- Remove the spark plug.
- Screw a compression gauge into the plug hole.
- Pull the starter cord 4-5 times. Note peak reading.
- Healthy 4-stroke: 90–120+ psi. Below 60 psi = ring wear, valve seat leak, or blown head gasket.
- Two-stroke: 90–110+ psi. Below 60 psi = same causes as 4-stroke, plus reed valve failure.

## Spark test

- Remove the plug. Connect the plug wire to the plug. Ground the plug body against a bare metal engine surface.
- Pull the cord. Look for a bright blue spark at the electrode gap.
- No spark = ignition coil, kill switch, or magneto issue.
- Weak orange spark = ignition system failing, will run poorly.

## Fuel

- Fresh gasoline in the tank? (Old ethanol fuel over 90 days = phase-separated water at the bottom of the tank.)
- Fuel reaching the carburetor? (Loosen the carb bowl drain; expect fuel to flow.)
- Fuel reaching the cylinder? (Choke ON, prime, pull; slight fuel smell at plug = yes.)
