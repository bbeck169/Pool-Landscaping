# Module 3 — Small Engine Repair · Chapter 7: Field diagnostics + escalation

*Original in-house authorship. Consult the OEM manual for model-specific fault trees.*

## Field workflow

A crew showing up with a dead engine has three options:

1. **Fix on-site.** Fuel + plug + air filter + carb aerosol cover 60-70% of failures. Truck stock should include: fresh fuel container, spark plugs (2 common part numbers), air filter (2 common part numbers), carb spray, universal fuel line, spark tester, compression gauge.
2. **Swap for a spare unit.** Larger crews keep 1 spare of each critical tool. Broken unit goes to shop for full repair; work continues uninterrupted.
3. **Send to shop.** Anything beyond field-diagnose scope — internal engine work, ignition coil replacement, gear-case rebuild.

**Time budget.** Don't spend more than 20 minutes trying to fix an engine on-site. Every minute past that is time the crew isn't cutting grass. If it's not fixed in 20 min, swap the unit and move on.

## Escalation triggers

- **Low compression** (<60 psi). Internal engine work; shop.
- **Broken recoil housing.** Crack in plastic. Shop or replace.
- **Bent crankshaft.** Mower blade hit a rock. Shop or replace engine.
- **Any smoke from the crankcase seal** on a 4-stroke. Shop.
- **Warranty repair on new equipment.** Send to OEM-authorized service center, not internal shop. Keeps warranty valid.

## Fleet management

- Log every repair against a specific tool ID.
- After 3 major repairs in 12 months on one tool = retire it. The math on continued repair rarely wins vs. replacement.
- Track hours (if the engine has an hour meter) — service intervals should be hour-based, not calendar-based.

## Customer conversation

Landscape customers understand that gasoline equipment breaks. What they don't understand is why the tech left mid-visit. The framing that works: "Your equipment worked fine; my equipment broke. I have a spare tool coming from the truck to finish this visit; the broken tool goes to the shop tonight and is back in service tomorrow." Never make it the customer's problem.
