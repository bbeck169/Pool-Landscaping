# Module 3 — Small Engine Repair · Chapter 6: Two-stroke specifics

*Original in-house authorship. Consult the OEM manual for 2-stroke mix ratios + oil types.*

Two-stroke engines are simpler than 4-strokes — no oil sump, no valve train, half the power strokes per revolution. They compensate by running at 8,000-14,000 RPM and burning oil-in-fuel for internal lubrication.

## The mix ratio matters

- 50:1 is the modern standard (Echo, Stihl, most current tools). One 2.6 oz bottle of oil per gallon of gasoline.
- 40:1 is common on older equipment.
- 32:1 or 25:1 shows up on vintage / commercial. Consult OEM.

**Never guess.** Wrong ratio = seized piston (too little oil) or fouled plug + smoke + power loss (too much oil).

## Common failures

- **Fouled spark plug.** Wet, oily plug. Clean and dry, or replace. Usually caused by:
  - Choke left on too long
  - Fuel too rich (mixture screw setting)
  - Bad plug or wrong plug heat range
- **Won't rev up.** Clogged spark arrestor screen in the muffler (blow it out or replace) or dirty carburetor.
- **Cuts out under load.** Fuel filter in tank clogged (2-stroke tanks have a small filter on the fuel pickup weight). Replace.
- **Melted piston skirt.** Ran on straight gas (no oil) by accident. Terminal — replace the piston + cylinder assembly or the engine.

## Fuel line degradation

Ethanol fuel eats the plastic fuel lines on 2-stroke engines faster than any other component. Every 2-3 years, inspect the primer bulb + all lines for cracks. Replacement kit is $5-10.

## Storage

Drain the tank before storage or run the engine dry. Old fuel + 2-stroke oil + heat + humidity = varnish that seizes the carburetor in a month.

## Trimmer head + chain-saw chain wear

Wear items outside the engine itself — but a tech doing a 2-stroke service should always visually inspect these and quote replacement parts to the customer.
