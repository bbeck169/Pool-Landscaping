# Module 3 — Small Engine Repair · Chapter 1: The small-engine landscape

*Original in-house authorship. Honda, Briggs & Stratton, Kohler, Kawasaki, Echo, Stihl cited by name + external OEM manual link only.*

A landscape crew maintains a fleet of small internal-combustion engines: mowers, string trimmers, edgers, blowers, chainsaws, aerators, sod cutters. Two engine families dominate:

- **4-stroke** — separate oil sump, gasoline in the tank. Used on mowers, pressure washers, most walk-behind equipment. Honda GX / GC series, Briggs & Stratton, Kohler are the common brands.
- **2-stroke** — oil pre-mixed with gasoline (typically 50:1 ratio). Used on trimmers, blowers, chainsaws — anywhere the engine needs to run at any orientation. Echo, Stihl, Husqvarna dominate.

**Concept, not menu.** This module teaches diagnostic patterns that apply to any brand. For torque specs, fuel-mixture ratios, or model-specific rebuild kits, always consult the OEM manual for the specific engine on the bench.

## Safety upfront

- Every small engine must have its spark plug wire pulled before any wrench touches it. A blade rotation can start a warm engine without turning the ignition switch.
- Fuel handling: gas outdoors only, never near sparks, always in an approved fuel container.
- Muffler exhausts reach 800°F+. Never grab a hot engine.
- Two-stroke fuel smells sweet; don't sip. Ethanol-blended fuel attacks fuel lines and carburetors — recommend non-ethanol premium for any engine in seasonal storage.
