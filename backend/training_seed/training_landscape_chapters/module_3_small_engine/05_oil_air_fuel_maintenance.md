# Module 3 — Small Engine Repair · Chapter 5: Oil, air, and fuel maintenance

*Original in-house authorship. Consult the OEM manual for exact oil weight + change intervals.*

## 4-stroke oil

- Check the dipstick before every use. Full-service intervals typically 25-50 hours of operation.
- Use the OEM-specified weight (typically SAE 30 in warm climates, 10W-30 in variable). Never use 2-stroke oil in a 4-stroke sump.
- Drain warm oil for best flow. Refill with a measured amount (OEM manual gives capacity).
- Check the level after refill BEFORE starting. Overfilled = seals blow.

## Air filter

- Foam element: wash in soapy water, dry completely, re-oil lightly with foam-filter oil, wring out excess. Reinstall.
- Paper element: tap out debris. Replace when visibly soaked with oil or too dirty to shake clean. Do not blow with compressed air — you'll blow holes in the media.
- Dual-stage filters have both; service both.

**Missing air filter kills engines fast.** Field dust + sand goes straight into the cylinder, scores the bore, wrecks the rings within hours. Never run an engine without its filter, even "just to see if it starts."

## Fuel management

- Ethanol-free premium is worth the premium price for any engine you can't run through in 30 days.
- Fuel stabilizer (Sta-Bil or equivalent) extends fuel shelf life to 6-12 months. Add to the tank + run 5 minutes to circulate before storage.
- Fuel filter (inline plastic can) — replace annually or when visibly discolored.
- 2-stroke mix — measure carefully; too little oil seizes the piston, too much fouls plugs. 50:1 is the most common ratio; verify per OEM.

## Blade sharpening + balance

For mowers: sharpen at 40 hours or every quarter, whichever comes first. Balance with a magnetic balancer or a nail through the center hole. Unbalanced blade = shot bearings within a season.
