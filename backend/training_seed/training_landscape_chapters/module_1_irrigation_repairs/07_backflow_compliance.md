# Module 1 — Irrigation Repairs · Chapter 7: Backflow preventers and code compliance

*Original in-house authorship. Cite local plumbing code for jurisdiction-specific requirements.*

The backflow preventer stops irrigation water — which can carry fertilizer, pesticide, and soil contaminants — from siphoning back into the domestic water line during a pressure drop. **This is a life-safety device, not an optional part.**

## Types you'll see in the field

- **PVB (Pressure Vacuum Breaker).** Most common on residential. Above-grade, hood-shaped brass device on the mainline just downstream of the POC. Must be installed at least 12" above the highest downstream head.
- **RPZ (Reduced Pressure Zone).** Required on commercial, on any system with chemical injection, or anywhere local code demands. Larger, has two check valves and a relief port.
- **DCV (Double Check Valve).** Some jurisdictions allow these for lower-risk residential. Less common.

## Annual test requirement

Most jurisdictions require an **annual certified backflow test** by a licensed tester. This is not a repair item — it's a compliance record. The tenant should track test dates per customer and remind customers 30 days before the deadline.

## Repair triggers

- Discharge from the relief port on an RPZ. Means an internal check has failed. Replace immediately — the system is unprotected right now.
- Ice damage after freeze. PVBs crack in the first freeze if not blown out for winter (Chapter 8 topic in a future module).
- Vandalism / theft (brass has salvage value).

## When to call the plumber

Any repair on the pressurized side of the backflow — or any test failure — escalates to a licensed backflow-certified plumber. **Do not attempt to bypass a failed backflow "just to keep the customer's grass green."** That's a public-health violation with real legal consequences for both the tech and the tenant.

## Documentation

Every backflow repair: device model, serial number, part(s) replaced, next test-due date. Keep the annual test certificate on file — municipalities audit these.
