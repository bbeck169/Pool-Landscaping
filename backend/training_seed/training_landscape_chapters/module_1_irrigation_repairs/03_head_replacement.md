# Module 1 — Irrigation Repairs · Chapter 3: Head replacement — spray, rotor, drip

*Original in-house authorship. Hunter, Rain Bird, Toro heads referenced by name + external OEM manual link only.*

## Pop-up spray heads

The most common failure: chewed-up nozzle, cracked body from a lawnmower, or a sunk head hidden under Bermuda thatch.

Replacement:
1. Dig around the head with a hand trowel until you expose the top of the body and the threaded riser below.
2. Unscrew the head from the riser (counter-clockwise from above).
3. Screw on the new head. Do not use pipe tape on plastic head threads — the threads are self-sealing by design; tape traps grit.
4. Backfill loose soil, tamp lightly. The nozzle should sit **flush with grade**, not proud.
5. Adjust the arc + radius per the customer's zone layout.

## Rotor heads

Rotors have a gear-driven mechanism to sweep in an arc. Failures: gear seizure (won't turn), arc drift (creeps out of adjustment), stuck pop-up (broken retract spring).

Replacement is identical to spray heads but the body is larger. **Always match the same brand and model on replacement** — arc-adjustment mechanisms are not cross-compatible. Note the model number molded into the top of the cap before ordering.

## Drip emitters

Drip point emitters plug from mineral deposits and root intrusion. Replace with a matching flow-rated emitter (0.5, 1, 2 GPH). Don't upsize as a "fix" for underwatering — that's a design problem, not a repair.

**Rebuild vs replace decision.** If the head body is intact and only the internal cartridge is worn, replace the cartridge (available for higher-end Hunter and Rain Bird models). If the body is cracked or the riser threads are stripped, replace the whole head.
