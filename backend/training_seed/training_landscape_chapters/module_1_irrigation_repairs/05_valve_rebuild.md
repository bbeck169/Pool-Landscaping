# Module 1 — Irrigation Repairs · Chapter 5: Valve rebuild and solenoid swap

*Original in-house authorship. Hunter, Rain Bird, Toro valves cited by name + link only.*

Zone valves have two failure modes and a rebuild kit for each.

## Solenoid failure

**Symptom:** controller sends 24 VAC to the valve, no click, no water. Solenoid open-circuit or shorted.

**Test:** disconnect the two solenoid leads at the controller. Measure resistance across the pair (long meter leads back at the valve, easier). Healthy solenoids read 20–60 ohms. Reading over ~200 ohms or infinite = open-circuit; reading below 10 ohms = shorted.

**Fix:** unscrew the solenoid from the valve body (counter-clockwise). Screw a replacement in hand-tight — don't over-torque, the threaded boss on the valve is soft plastic and will crack.

## Diaphragm failure

**Symptom:** solenoid clicks, valve doesn't open OR won't shut off. The rubber diaphragm inside is torn, folded, or seized with debris.

**Fix:** unscrew the top of the valve body. Lift out the diaphragm. Inspect for tears. Clean any grit from the seat. Install a fresh diaphragm from the rebuild kit for that valve model. Reseat the top, torque per manufacturer spec (usually hand-tight is fine).

## The "weeps forever" symptom

Valve neither fully open nor fully closed. Usually a grain of grit lodged on the diaphragm seat. Sometimes solves with a manual bleed of the valve (unscrew the top and quickly re-tighten to let water flush the seat). If it persists, replace the diaphragm.

## Don't lubricate the diaphragm

Silicone, petroleum, anything — the diaphragm is designed to seal dry against the seat. Lubricant attracts grit and shortens life.
