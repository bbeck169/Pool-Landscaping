# Module 1 — Irrigation Repairs · Chapter 4: Lateral line repair

*Original in-house authorship.*

Broken laterals show up as wet spots, geysers, or the plumbing equivalent of a sinkhole under the lawn. Almost always PVC or poly pipe cut by a shovel, a rock, or a tree root.

## PVC lateral (rigid, glued)

1. Shut the zone valve. Confirm no water at the head.
2. Dig around the break to expose 12" of pipe on each side.
3. Wait a few minutes for standing water to drain.
4. Cut out the damaged section with a PVC cutter or hacksaw.
5. Deburr both cut ends with a knife or utility blade.
6. Dry the pipe ends. Solvent cement will not bond wet PVC — this is the #1 cause of repair callbacks.
7. Install a slip-slip repair coupling (or a longer patch section with two couplings, if the gap is > 6"). Use standard PVC solvent cement + primer per the cement manufacturer's cure time.
8. Wait the full cure time before pressurizing. Rushing this creates a leaking joint that fails within days.

## Poly lateral (flexible, barbed)

1. Shut zone valve.
2. Cut out damaged section.
3. Warm the pipe ends with sun or a hand towel soaked in warm water — cold poly refuses barbs.
4. Insert barbed coupling. Cinch each side with a stainless clamp.
5. Pressurize. Poly repairs can be tested immediately — no cure time.

## Callback avoidance

If the break is at a fitting (elbow, tee), replace the fitting, not just the pipe on either side. Fittings that broke once are stressed and will re-break.
