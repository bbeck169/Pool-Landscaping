# Module 1 — Irrigation Repairs · Chapter 6: Wire tracing and multi-strand faults

*Original in-house authorship.*

Irrigation wire is direct-burial 18 AWG multi-strand. One conductor is the **common** shared by every valve; one conductor per zone runs from the controller to that specific valve's solenoid.

## Common failure modes

- **Cut common wire.** Every zone stops responding. Diagnostic: at the controller, all zones read the same fault.
- **Cut zone wire.** One zone stops responding.
- **Nicked insulation touching wet soil.** Random cross-talk — a zone triggers when a different zone runs.
- **Corroded splice.** A splice buried without a waterproof splice kit corrodes over 1–3 years; resistance rises; solenoid pulls in weakly or not at all.

## Wire-tracing tools

- **Continuity tester + long jumper cable.** Old school; works.
- **Solenoid activator** (buzzes a target solenoid so you can hear it in the ground).
- **Wire toner** (radio-frequency signal + receiver wand). Best tool for finding underground splices.

## Splice best practice

- Use a waterproof splice kit — gel-filled crimp or heat-shrink solder — every time. **Wire nuts are not acceptable outdoors.**
- Strip 3/4" of insulation, twist the copper strands together tightly, apply crimp/solder, apply the waterproofing sleeve.
- Bury the splice inside a valve box, not directly in dirt, so future techs can find it.

## Escalation

If the fault is under a driveway, patio, or established hardscape, escalate to a licensed underground trench crew before you dig. Cost is $200–500 for a properly-permitted directional bore vs. thousands in repair for cutting a gas or fiber line.
