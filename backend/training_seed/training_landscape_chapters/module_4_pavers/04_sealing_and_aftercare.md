# Pavers & Hardscape — Sealing and Aftercare

**Original in-house authorship. Vendor products cited by name/link only.**

Sealing a paver installation is optional but often requested. A sealer adds visual richness (a wet-look enhancement or a natural matte finish), protects against oil and organic staining, and locks polymeric joint sand in place against wash-out. Sealer is not a repair — a badly installed field can't be sealed into stability — but on a properly installed field, sealer meaningfully extends the interval between service visits.

Wait at least sixty to ninety days after install before sealing. Fresh pavers release efflorescence — a chalky whitish haze from mineral salts in the concrete migrating to the surface — and sealing over efflorescence traps it under the film where it becomes permanent. Let the initial efflorescence complete, brush or acid-wash it off, then seal.

Two sealer families dominate the market. Solvent-based acrylic sealers penetrate deeper, produce a longer-lasting film, and often generate the wet-look enhancement people ask for. Water-based sealers are lower-VOC, easier to clean up, produce a softer natural finish, and are the required choice in some jurisdictions.

Apply on a dry day with temperatures between fifty and eighty degrees Fahrenheit. The paver surface must be bone dry — check by taping a piece of plastic sheeting to the surface for ten minutes; if the underside of the plastic is dry, you're clear to seal. Apply with a low-pressure garden sprayer in overlapping passes, avoiding puddles and drips. Back-roll with a lambswool roller on driveways to work the sealer evenly into the field.

Sealer service life depends on exposure. Full-sun south-facing driveways in dry climates typically need re-sealing every two to three years. Shaded patios in wetter climates stretch that to four to five. Always re-seal in the same product family — a solvent-based topcoat over a water-based sealer, or vice versa, will lift and bubble.

Aftercare guidance to give the customer:
- Sweep or blow debris weekly to keep organic matter from staining.
- Rinse with a garden hose; do not use a pressure washer on tips higher than 25 degrees or closer than twelve inches to the surface, especially over polymeric joints.
- For oil or grease stains, treat within twenty-four hours with a paver-safe degreaser, not household bleach or ammonia.
- Any settled or heaved paver should be pulled and reset within the first year; hold the matching paver stock you set aside during install for exactly this purpose.

Field tip: sealer applied over a damp field will fog milky-white as it cures. Once it does that, the only fix is a solvent-based stripping product followed by re-sealing — a full day of extra work on top of the original job. Confirm dryness with the plastic-sheet test every time.
