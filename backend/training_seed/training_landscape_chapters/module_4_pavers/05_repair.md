# Pavers & Hardscape — Repair of Settled or Heaved Pavers

**Original in-house authorship. Vendor products cited by name/link only.**

Even a well-installed field will develop the occasional problem paver. Settled pavers sink below the surrounding field, usually from a small void in the bedding sand or a compacted subgrade weakness. Heaved pavers rise above the field, usually from frost, root pressure, or a shifting base rock course underneath. Both are repaired using the same lift-and-reset approach; the only real difference is diagnosis.

Start with observation. Walk the field and mark every problem paver with chalk. Note the direction of failure (settled or heaved), the extent (one paver or a cluster), and any adjacent condition — a nearby tree root, an unmapped downspout, an obviously undersized base course visible at the edge.

Lift the problem paver using two flat-head screwdrivers or purpose-made paver extraction tongs. Pry from opposing corners, being careful not to chip the surrounding chamfers. Once the first paver is out, adjacent pavers lift easily. Pull enough pavers to expose the bedding sand and, ideally, the base rock underneath the failure point. Photograph the excavation before you disturb it — you'll want that photo for the customer file and for warranty documentation.

Diagnose the cause. Common findings and fixes:

**Bedding sand washed away** — visible cavity or a low spot in the sand. Common cause: nearby downspout dumping water directly into the paver edge. Fix by adding new bedding sand, screeding level, and if the downspout is the culprit, extending it away from the field with a splash block or extension.

**Base rock compacted into a low spot** — the base rock has densified and dropped. Fix by adding coarse aggregate to restore the target elevation, plate-compacting the new material, then rescreeding bedding sand.

**Root intrusion under a heaved paver** — visible root pushing up. Fix by cutting the offending root (with the customer's OK — some roots are structural to the tree) and back-filling with coarse aggregate. If the root is too large to cut without harming the tree, escalate to the customer: relocate the paver run, or plan for periodic re-lift service.

**Subgrade failure** — deep soft spot below the base rock. Rare but expensive. Requires re-excavating to firm subgrade, adding geotextile fabric, and rebuilding the base. Get the customer's approval before proceeding; this is a change-order-scale job.

Reset pavers in the same layout you found them, sweep in fresh joint sand, and mist-water polymeric sand if the field was originally set with it.

Field diagnostic tip: keep a small "paver repair kit" in the truck — screwdrivers, extraction tongs, a bag of bedding sand, a bag of joint sand, and a bagful of matching pavers from the customer's original job. Most single-paver repairs are a fifteen-minute stop when you're prepared.
