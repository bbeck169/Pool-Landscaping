# Turf & Sod — Post-Install Watering and Establishment

**Original in-house authorship. Vendor products cited by name/link only.**

The two weeks after install decide whether your sod becomes lawn or becomes callback. This chapter is about the watering schedule that gets rolls to root, and how to teach the customer to stretch that schedule as the lawn matures.

**Days 0-14 — establishment phase.** New sod has almost no root reach — three-quarters of an inch at best. That means the shallow root zone dries out in hours during warm weather. Set the controller to water twice per day for the first two weeks: once in the morning (around 6 a.m.) and once in early afternoon (around 1 p.m. — before the peak heat but late enough that morning water has dissipated). Each cycle should apply enough water to keep the top two inches of soil dark and cool to the touch, but not so much that puddles form or water runs off. On typical residential systems this ends up around 10 minutes per zone twice daily, but the calibration is done by walking the yard after a cycle and checking the soil — not by trusting the timer.

Warm-weather adjustments: if daytime highs are consistently above 95 °F or wind is above 10 mph, add a third short mid-morning cycle (around 10 a.m.) of about half the duration. This is a bridge cycle to prevent midday burn on the exposed edges of the pallet.

**Days 15-28 — transition phase.** Roots have knit lightly into the underlying soil. Now the goal is to train the roots to grow deeper. Reduce to once per day (still morning) for the first week of this phase, then to every other day for the second week. Increase run time on each watering to compensate — the total weekly water applied should stay about the same, but delivered in fewer, deeper events.

**Day 29 onward — mature schedule.** Roots are now three inches deep or deeper. Shift to two or three watering days per week, again in early morning, with enough run time to wet the top four to six inches of soil. This is the schedule the customer will keep long-term. Cool-season turf will use slightly less water; warm-season turf can go slightly longer between events but wants deep soaks.

**First mow.** Wait until roots have knit firmly enough that a gentle tug on a corner of a roll doesn't lift it — usually 3-4 weeks. Mow with a sharp blade, at the highest setting your mower supports, in dry conditions. Never remove more than a third of the blade height in a single mow — the one-third rule applies from day one and forever after. Bagging the first two mows keeps clippings from smothering still-rooting turf.

**Fertilizer program.** Wait 3-4 weeks after install before applying a follow-up fertilizer. Starter blend went down at install; the follow-up is a balanced turf fertilizer per label rate. Do not fertilize during the first three weeks — young roots will burn.

**Watching for trouble.** Yellow stripes along seam edges usually mean gaps or dry seams — the fix is hand-watering the seams for a week. Yellow patches in the middle of rolls usually mean the roll dried on the pallet before install or that a puddling controller isn't reaching that spot. Grey-green wilt across the entire lawn means too little water; a mushy soft feel underfoot means too much.

Field tip: hand the customer a one-page schedule at install. Print it in the truck cab if you have to. The single biggest cause of establishment failure isn't technique — it's the customer overwatering out of anxiety in week 4 and drowning the freshly knitted roots.
