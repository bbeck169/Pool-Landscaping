# Turf & Sod — Install Prep and Laying Technique

**Original in-house authorship. Vendor products cited by name/link only.**

Once site prep is done and the soil is graded, tested, and lightly fertilized, install day comes down to one goal — get every square inch of the roll's underside in firm contact with moist soil, with staggered joints, before the sod dries out. Everything else is variation on that theme.

Start the layout from the longest straight edge of the project — usually the sidewalk, driveway, or patio edge. Rolling a straight seam along a hardscape and squaring off the rest of the field from there gives you a clean visual line and reduces cutting waste. Lay the first row full-length end-to-end, butted tightly against the hardscape. Do not overlap onto pavement or against masonry — sod that touches concrete or brick dries from that edge outward and yellows within days.

Butt each roll against its neighbor with zero gap and zero overlap. Gaps of even a half inch dry into visible seam lines within two weeks. Overlaps cause the raised piece to die and leave a strip of dead grass. If a roll has a torn or ragged edge, cut it straight with a sharp sod knife or hand saw before laying it.

Stagger the rows like brickwork. Each subsequent row should start with a half-length or two-thirds-length piece so the joints between rows do not line up. Aligned joints create a continuous seam that dries out, is hard to water evenly, and shows as a long yellow stripe in the first month.

For curved edges and around obstacles (sprinkler heads, tree wells, valve boxes), lay full rolls and cut in place. Trim with a sharp sod knife using a smooth pulling motion, keeping the blade tilted slightly under the roll so the cut edge tucks tightly against whatever you're contouring around. Save large offcuts — they usually fit into the next row's short end.

Slopes need special handling. On any grade steeper than about one-in-six, run rolls perpendicular to the fall line (across the slope, not up and down it) and stake the top edge of each roll with a wooden or biodegradable stake every three to four feet. Without staking, wet sod slides downhill within hours of the first watering. Remove the stakes after two weeks once roots have knit into the soil.

Once the field is laid, roll the entire installation with a lawn roller half-filled with water. This presses the sod into intimate contact with the soil below — the single most important mechanical step for root establishment. Skip this and you'll see the outline of every roll edge for months.

Water immediately. From the moment the first roll goes down, the clock is running. On summer install days, be watering the earliest-laid rolls even while the crew is still placing the last ones on the far side of the yard. Sod that sits dry on soil for even an hour in warm weather loses a significant percentage of the shallow roots that make establishment possible.

Field tip: the two-week check. Walk the install at day 14 and gently tug a corner of each roll. If it lifts easily, the rolls behind it are not rooting either — usually a watering coverage problem. If it holds firm, you're on track for a healthy establishment.
