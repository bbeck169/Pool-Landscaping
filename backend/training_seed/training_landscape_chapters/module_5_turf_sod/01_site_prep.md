# Turf & Sod — Site Preparation and Soil

**Original in-house authorship. Vendor products cited by name/link only.**

Great sod comes off the pallet as a nearly perfect product. Two thirds of the failures we see are baked in before the first roll ever lands — bad site prep. The customer sees dying strips and blames the sod farm, but the root cause is almost always soil.

Start by killing what's already there. If the area is weeds and old lawn, apply a non-selective herbicide per label directions and wait the recommended interval (typically 7-14 days) before disturbing the soil. This is the single most important step for weed-free establishment. Rushing this step is why new sod comes back yellow-and-weedy in month three.

After die-off, remove the dead material and any hardscape debris. Rototill the top four to six inches to break up compacted soil and improve root penetration. If the customer's existing soil is heavy clay or pure sand, amend with three to four cubic yards of quality compost per thousand square feet, tilled evenly into the top four inches. Skip this step and the sod's shallow root system will hit the untouched compacted layer and stall.

Grade the site with a landscape rake, aiming for a smooth surface sloped away from the house at a minimum of one eighth inch per foot. Break up clods larger than an inch across; they'll create air pockets under the sod. Roll the finished grade lightly with a lawn roller half-filled with water to firm the seedbed without over-compacting it. Your footprint should leave a shallow impression, not sink in.

Test soil pH before installing sod. Cool-season turf (tall fescue, Kentucky bluegrass, ryegrass) prefers pH 6.0-7.0. Warm-season turf (bermuda, zoysia, St. Augustine) tolerates 5.5-7.5. If the pH is out of range, apply lime (to raise pH) or sulfur (to lower pH) per soil-test recommendation and till in.

Just before delivery, apply a starter fertilizer high in phosphorus per label rates. Water the prepared bed lightly to settle the surface. Time your sod delivery for the same day or next-day install — sod that sits stacked on pallets for more than 24 hours in warm weather starts to heat and yellow from the inside of the pallet.

Field tip: keep a simple checklist in the truck: herbicide date noted → till depth confirmed → amendment added → graded and rolled → pH tested → starter fertilizer down → sod arrival scheduled. Any skipped step becomes a callback later.
