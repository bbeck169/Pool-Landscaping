# Module D — Jandy O-rings · Chapter 6: Check valves and specialty gaskets

*Original in-house authorship.*

Check valves are a different animal. Most Jandy inline check valves use a **flap** (or a spring-loaded shuttle) held in place with a lid and a body-to-lid O-ring or gasket.

**Field signs of a failing check valve gasket:**
- Water dripping from the seam where lid meets body while the pump runs.
- Air being drawn in on the suction side of a check valve (bubbles at the pump basket after the check valve is in the suction line).
- The check no longer holds prime overnight — the flap is fine but the housing gasket is bypassing.

**Rebuild path:**
1. Pump OFF, water lowered on the isolated section.
2. Remove the lid clamp band (or the lid screws, model-dependent).
3. Lift the lid. Inspect the flap: hairline cracks along the hinge, calcium buildup on the sealing edge, or a flap that no longer springs closed on its own = replace the flap.
4. Remove the body-to-lid O-ring or flat gasket. Discard.
5. Wipe both sealing surfaces clean.
6. Install fresh O-ring (Teflon-safe silicone lube; light film).
7. Replace the flap if needed — some kits have color-coded flaps for flow-direction sanity.
8. Reseat the lid. Reinstall clamp or screws to spec. Restart pump. Check for leak.

**Specialty note:** JVAs (Jandy Valve Actuators) — the little electric motors on top of the diverters for automated systems — have their own weather boot at the bottom that ages faster than the valve itself. If a customer reports "the valve doesn't turn anymore," check the actuator drive coupling and the boot before assuming the underlying diverter is the problem.
