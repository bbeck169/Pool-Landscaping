# Module D — Jandy O-rings · Chapter 2: Failure signatures

*Original in-house authorship. Jandy referenced by name + link only.*

Three symptoms bring you out for a valve rebuild.

**Weeping at the stem.** Water tracking down the outside of the valve body from around the diverter handle. This is the shaft O-ring on the stem. Small drip = still catchable; puddle = the shaft is grooved and you'll be replacing the stem, not just the O-ring.

**Cross-flow between ports.** Customer reports water flowing to the wrong return or the wrong feature (spa mode fills the pool, floor cleaner runs while the spa is on). This is the diverter seal — the flat gasket on the rotating disc that mates with the stationary port face. Cross-flow means the seal has hardened or torn.

**Slow drip at the bonnet.** Water forming at the seam between the bonnet (top cap) and the valve body. This is the main body O-ring. Overtightened bonnet screws will crush this O-ring; under-tightened will let it weep.

Never over-torque a Jandy bonnet screw looking for a leak — the screws are self-locating and the O-ring seats itself. Extra torque splits the plastic bonnet.
