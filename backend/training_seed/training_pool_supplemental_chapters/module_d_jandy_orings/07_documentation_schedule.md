# Module D — Jandy O-rings · Chapter 7: Documentation + preventive schedule

*Original in-house authorship.*

Every valve rebuild should log: **valve type/model, symptom that brought you out, parts installed (kit number + any additional parts), test outcome (leak/no leak at 60 sec run + 24 hr later), next recommended service date.**

**Preventive schedule.** In desert climates (Vistancia and similar), pool water chemistry pushes seal life to the low end of the range because scale, chloramines, and UV all age the elastomers. A working assumption:

- NeverLube diverter O-rings: rebuild every 3–5 years.
- Bonnet O-rings: replaced at every rebuild.
- Diverter face seals: replaced at every rebuild.
- Check valve flap + O-ring set: rebuild every 4–6 years or on any priming complaint.
- JVA actuators: expected life 5–7 years; boot inspect at every rebuild.

**Customer conversation.** When a customer sees $60–120 in parts for a valve rebuild, they push back. The framing that works: a full rebuild is $100–200 in parts and 45 min of labor; a valve failure that floods a basement or drains the pool overnight is a five-figure claim. The rebuild is insurance, not maintenance.

**Warranty note.** Jandy honors kit-part warranty when the OE part number appears on the invoice. Photograph the box and part number before installing — if you get a bad seal in the field, you have the evidence for the warranty claim.

**Inventory recommendation.** Every service truck should carry one of each: NeverLube diverter rebuild kit, standard bonnet O-ring, check-valve flap-and-gasket kit. Cost is under $50 per truck. Saves a whole return trip when a random rebuild request comes in.
