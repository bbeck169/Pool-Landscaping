# Module D — Jandy O-rings · Chapter 4: Diverter valve teardown

*Original in-house authorship.*

1. Pump OFF and locked. Confirm.
2. Remove the two Phillips screws holding the handle to the diverter shaft. Set aside on a clean rag.
3. Remove the bonnet screws (typically 6 or 8 around the perimeter, depending on model year). They may be Phillips or 5/16" hex head.
4. Lift the bonnet straight up off the valve body. If it resists, wiggle side-to-side gently. Do not pry — you'll crack the flange.
5. Lift the diverter assembly out of the body. Note the orientation of the diverter port relative to the body ports.
6. Inspect the interior. Common findings:
   - **Sand or grit in the sealing surfaces** — flush with clean water before proceeding.
   - **Calcium ring** on the diverter seal — indicates high pH exposure or old age.
   - **Cracked plastic port face** — order a new valve body; do not attempt a repair.
7. Remove the shaft O-rings from the stem (there are typically two, plus a small internal O-ring). Note orientation.
8. Remove the diverter face seal (flat gasket bonded to the disc — some models pop out, some are integral to a replacement disc).
9. Remove the bonnet O-ring from the bonnet-to-body groove. Wipe the groove clean.

All old rubber goes in the trash. All plastic gets cleaned with fresh water — no chemical cleaners on the plastic body. Bleach in particular attacks the plastic used in most valve bodies over time.
