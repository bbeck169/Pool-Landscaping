# Module A — Filter Cleaning · Chapter 6: O-rings, gaskets, seals, and torque

*Original in-house authorship. OEMs referenced by name + link only.*

## Why O-rings fail

Every filter housing has at least one main O-ring plus smaller gaskets on drain plugs, air-bleeds, and pressure-gauge ports. They fail from four causes:

1. **Wrong lube.** Petroleum grease attacks nitrile and EPDM rubber. Use Teflon-safe silicone lubricant only.
2. **UV degradation.** O-rings exposed to sunlight during a slow reassembly harden and crack. Keep parts in shade until reinstalling.
3. **Contamination.** Sand grain, hair, or dried DE crust on the sealing surface prevents even compression. Wipe every mating surface clean.
4. **Age.** Even a well-maintained O-ring hardens after 5–7 years. Log replacement dates in the service report.

## Gasket vs O-ring vs seal

- **O-ring**: round cross-section, sits in a machined groove, seals by radial compression.
- **Gasket**: flat sheet or shaped elastomer, seals by axial compression between two flanges.
- **Shaft seal**: dynamic seal on a rotating shaft — covered in Module B.

## The multiport diverter gasket (sand/DE)

The spider gasket inside the multiport valve is the single most common leak point on a sand filter. Signs of failure:

- Water dripping from the waste line during FILTER position
- Sand or DE in the pool with pump running
- Pressure that spikes when moving the handle

Replacement requires removing the valve stem, prying out the old gasket, cleaning the seat, seating a new gasket dry, and reassembling with cover-plate O-ring lubed. Do not glue the spider gasket — modern designs are dry-fit.

## Torque and clamp bands

Filter clamp bands seat against a housing O-ring. Overtightening compresses the O-ring beyond spec and shortens its life; undertightening leaks. Every OEM has a torque spec (typically 15–20 ft-lb for residential filters). Use a small torque wrench, not a hammer + screwdriver.

## Air-bleed check

Every filter has an air-bleed valve on top. After reassembly, open the bleed, start the pump, and let air escape until water streams. Close the bleed. Any hiss during operation means the housing O-ring isn't sealing — stop and re-seat.
