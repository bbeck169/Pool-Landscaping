# Module A — Filter Cleaning · Chapter 3: Backwash procedure (sand + DE)

*Original in-house authorship. No verbatim OEM content.*

## Prep — before you touch a valve

1. Test water chemistry and record readings. A pool that's about to be backwashed loses 3–5% of its water; you'll need to top off and rebalance.
2. Verify the waste line drain is clear and legally routed.
3. Confirm the pump is off. Multiport valve handles must NEVER be moved while the pump is running — the diverter gasket will shear.

## Sand backwash — step by step

1. Pump OFF.
2. Multiport valve → BACKWASH position.
3. Pump ON. Water flows out the waste line, carrying dislodged debris.
4. Watch the sight glass. Backwash until the water runs clear (typically 2–4 minutes).
5. Pump OFF.
6. Multiport valve → RINSE.
7. Pump ON for 20–30 seconds — settles the sand bed and prevents debris from re-entering the pool.
8. Pump OFF.
9. Multiport → FILTER.
10. Pump ON. Note the new clean-baseline pressure.

## DE backwash — differences

DE follows the same valve sequence but requires **DE recharge** at the end. Once the multiport is back on FILTER, the operator adds fresh DE powder through the skimmer (typically 1 lb DE per 5 sq ft of filter area). Without recharge, the DE grids are bare and won't filter properly.

## Common mistakes

- Backwashing too long → wastes water and lifts pea gravel or filter sand out of the tank.
- Not rinsing → sends dirty water back into the pool for the first minute after switching to FILTER.
- Moving the multiport handle with pump running → shears the spider gasket, requiring a valve rebuild.
- Backwashing more often than necessary → wastes water and loosens the sand bed prematurely.

## Sanitary discharge — check local code

Some municipalities require the waste line to discharge to sewer, not stormwater. Verify before the first backwash. Chlorinated water discharged to storm drains can trigger fines and kill downstream vegetation.
