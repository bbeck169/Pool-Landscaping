# Module A — Filter Cleaning · Chapter 7: Field diagnostics and troubleshooting

*Original in-house authorship. No verbatim OEM content.*

## The five-minute filter triage

When called out for "dirty water," diagnose the filter in this order:

1. **Read the gauge.** Compare to clean baseline. High = loaded media. Low = bypass leak.
2. **Check the sight glass or waste line.** During a short backwash, note water color and grit content.
3. **Feel the housing.** A cold filter with warm plumbing suggests low or no flow through the media.
4. **Listen at the pump.** A cavitation whine points at suction-side restriction, not filter media.
5. **Look at the returns.** Air bubbles = suction leak. DE dust = torn grid. Sand grain = torn lateral.

## Symptom → cause → fix map

- **Rising pressure over days** → media loading normally → clean/backwash.
- **Rising pressure after a rain** → skimmer basket full or algae in suspension → basket first, then chemistry.
- **Pressure spike immediately after backwash + recharge** → over-charged DE or restrictor plate stuck → recheck DE quantity.
- **Dropping pressure with clear water** → sand channeling or filter bypass valve open → inspect internals.
- **DE dust in pool** → torn grid → schedule teardown.
- **Sand in pool** → cracked lateral or standpipe → schedule teardown.
- **Pool water cloudy despite clean filter** → chemistry (usually low sanitizer or high combined chlorine) → chemistry-first, not filter.

## Water clarity vs filter performance

A filter that runs at spec but returns cloudy water almost always has a chemistry problem — not a filter problem. Common culprits: high combined chlorine, low alkalinity crashing pH and dissolving surface calcium, saltcell scale rebounding phosphates into the water. Fix chemistry first, then re-evaluate the filter.

## When to recommend replacement

- Cartridge elements: pleat collapse or torn end-cap.
- Sand: bed contaminated with oil/calcium and won't rinse clear — refill with fresh sand.
- DE grids: any tear that won't repair with grid-tape or replacement fabric.
- Multiport valve: leaking after 2+ diverter gasket replacements.
- Housing: cracked, sun-crazed, or rusted through the clamp seat.

## Report writing

Every filter service should log: **pressure_pre, pressure_post, media_type, media_age, cleaning_method, DE_amount_if_applicable, replacement_parts_installed, next_recommended_service.** This is how the tenant tracks fleet-wide media life and avoids blind replacement.
