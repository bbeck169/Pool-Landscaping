# Module B — Shaft Seal · Chapter 7: When to replace the whole motor

*Original in-house authorship.*

Some field conditions make a seal-only repair a bad investment. Recommend a whole-motor replacement when:

- **Shaft is grooved** where the old seal ran. New seal will not seat. Replace motor.
- **Endbell is rusted through** from a long undetected seep. Structural failure imminent.
- **Bearings feel gritty** or roar when spun by hand. Bearings are wet; motor life is <30 days.
- **Motor age >8 years** and running at reduced efficiency (drawing higher amps than nameplate). Replacement pays back in energy savings.
- **Same seal has failed twice** in <18 months. Likely a shaft alignment or vibration issue that keeps destroying seals. Fresh motor + alignment check.

**Customer conversation.** Explain the arithmetic: seal kit ≈ $30 + 45 min labor. Motor replacement ≈ $400 + 30 min labor. Doing the seal on a doomed motor means paying twice within the year. When any of the above conditions is present, replacement is the honest recommendation.

**Environmental note.** Old motors are recyclable — copper windings + steel housing. Some tenants have a scrap agreement with a local recycler that offsets replacement cost. Don't landfill them.
