# Module B — Shaft Seal · Chapter 5: Old seal removal + new seal installation

*Original in-house authorship.*

## Remove the old seal

The rotating half is on the impeller hub — usually a rubber cup around a carbon face. Pull it off with fingers; if stuck, gently pry with a plastic wedge.

The stationary half is pressed into the seal plate — usually a ceramic face in a rubber cup. Push it out from the back of the seal plate with your thumbs. If corroded in, tap gently with a wooden dowel and hammer.

## Clean the seats

Both the impeller hub bore and the seal-plate seat must be clean, dry, and free of any old seal debris. Wipe with a lint-free rag. Any grit on either seat = new seal fails in weeks.

## Install the new stationary half

Wet the outer rubber with clean water (never oil). Press evenly into the seal-plate bore with fingers or a soft dowel. The ceramic face must be **flat, level, and undamaged**. Any chip on the face = replace before assembly.

## Install the new rotating half

Wet the inner rubber with clean water. Slide onto the shaft, spring-side toward the motor, carbon-face away. Push down until it seats against the impeller hub.

## Reinstall the impeller

Hand-thread the impeller clockwise onto the shaft (verify direction per motor). The impeller compresses the rotating half against the stationary half — that's the seal. Snug hand-tight then a quarter-turn with a wrench flat. Do not over-torque.

## Reassemble

Reverse the disassembly order. Fresh volute O-ring, lubed with silicone. Fresh diffuser gasket if applicable. Torque volute clamp/bolts to OEM spec.
