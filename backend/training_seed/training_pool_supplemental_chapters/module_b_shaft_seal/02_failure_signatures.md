# Module B — Shaft Seal · Chapter 2: Failure signatures

*Original in-house authorship.*

Shaft seals fail in three patterns and each has a distinct field signature.

**Slow drip.** Water at the base of the motor, no motor damage yet. Usually caused by a worn face after 3–5 years of run time or minor chemistry burn from prolonged low-pH exposure. Replace at the next service.

**Sudden gush.** Water pouring from the shaft area, motor may still be running. Often caused by dry-running (pump lost prime and the seal faces overheated). Shut the pump down immediately; the seal is destroyed and the motor bearings may already be wet.

**Silent seepage.** No visible drip, but the motor endbell is rusting from inside. Discovered during motor replacement — the tell is a rust ring on the shaft where the seal ran. Replace both the seal and the motor.

If you see **any water track on the motor endbell, replace the seal.** Waiting adds a motor to the invoice.
