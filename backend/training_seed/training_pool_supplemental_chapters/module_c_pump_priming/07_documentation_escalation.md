# Module C — Pump Priming Diagnosis · Chapter 7: Documentation + escalation

*Original in-house authorship.*

Every priming diagnostic visit should produce a service report that captures:

- Pump make/model, motor HP, age (if known)
- Symptom category from Chapter 2 (which of the 5 patterns)
- Water level found (above / below skimmer weir midpoint)
- Skimmer basket condition
- Which suction-side joints were tested (soap-bubble or garden-hose)
- Test outcomes
- Parts installed (lid O-ring, check valve, unions, etc.)
- Whether the pump held prime on shutdown-and-restart before you left

**Escalation triggers.** Send the job to a senior tech or a licensed plumber when:

- Suspected underground plumbing leak (settled deck, wet spots near pipes)
- Repeated failure of the same joint after replacement
- Suspected cracked pump housing (not a repair — replace the pump)
- Any electrical smell or arcing at the motor

**Customer conversation.** If you find a chronic leak that will need a repair beyond the visit budget, quote the fix in writing before leaving. A homeowner who understands the diagnosis is a customer who authorizes the repair.

**Warranty note.** Pool pump manufacturers void warranty on any pump that has been run dry. Document any dry-run condition you find so the tenant's back-office knows to escalate to warranty claim vs. customer-pay repair.
