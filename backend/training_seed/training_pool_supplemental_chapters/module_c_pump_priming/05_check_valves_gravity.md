# Module C — Pump Priming Diagnosis · Chapter 5: Check valves and gravity systems

*Original in-house authorship.*

## Check-valve function

A check valve on the suction line holds water in the plumbing between service visits so the pump stays flooded overnight. Without one — or with a failed one — the water drains back to the pool through the impeller when the pump shuts off, leaving the volute empty at the next start.

## Signs of a failed check valve

- Pump primes fine when you fill the basket by hand, but loses prime overnight.
- You hear water gurgling back down the suction line 30 seconds after the pump shuts off.
- Sight-tube water level in the pump basket drops within minutes of shutdown.

## Testing without a rebuild

Close the suction valve manually right after the pump shuts off. If the pump then holds prime overnight, the check valve is the culprit — the suction valve is doing the check valve's job. Replace the check valve.

## Gravity systems

Some pools — usually older or with the equipment pad set below pool level — are **gravity-fed** and don't need a check valve because the pool water is above the pump elevation. On these systems, the pump should never lose prime unless there's an active leak. If a gravity-fed pump won't prime, the problem is either a suction-side blockage or a leak at or above the pool water level.

## Pumps mounted above pool level

Elevated equipment pads (roof pumps, hill-side pads) rely entirely on the check valve. On these systems, budget for check valve replacement every 5–7 years as preventive maintenance.
