# Module C — Pump Priming Diagnosis · Chapter 4: Suction-side air leak diagnosis

*Original in-house authorship.*

An air leak on the suction side of the pump is the most common cause of chronic priming trouble. On the suction side (skimmer → pump inlet), any gap draws air, not water, because the pump creates negative pressure there.

**Where to look — in priority order.**

1. **Pump basket lid.** Cracked lid, worn O-ring, or lid not threaded down all the way. Most common finding.
2. **Basket lid vent screw.** Small brass screw on top — over-tightened cracks the plastic; under-tightened weeps air.
3. **Suction-side unions.** Threaded union nuts between pump and plumbing. Check for cracked plastic and worn O-rings.
4. **Pump inlet fitting.** Female thread where the plumbing enters the pump. Check for hairline cracks around the boss.
5. **Suction diverter valve.** Multi-port diverters develop leaks at the top-cap gasket.
6. **Skimmer plumbing underground.** Rare, but possible on a settled deck.

**The soap-bubble test.** With the pump off and prime bled, spray a mild dish-soap solution around each joint. Start the pump; watch for bubbles being drawn in. It works because the small negative pressure at each joint pulls the soap film. It's the fastest field test for suction leaks.

**The garden-hose test.** With pump running and the pool topped off, run a garden hose over each suction-side joint. If a joint is leaking air, hosing it will briefly stop the priming problem — water can't be drawn in around the joint while the joint is under water.
