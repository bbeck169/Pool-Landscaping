# Module E — VSP Programming · Chapter 4: Measuring flow at the pad

*Original in-house authorship. Manufacturer keypads vary — reference the OEM manual for the specific model.*

To program to a flow target, you have to know the actual flow you're getting at a given RPM. Three options in order of preference:

**Option 1 — Built-in flow readout.** Some higher-end VSPs display an estimated GPM based on internal current + RPM math. Accuracy is ±10% but good enough for programming duties.

**Option 2 — Inline flow meter.** A physical flow meter installed on the return line. Blue-White is the market-standard brand; installation is a one-time labor charge and pays back the first year in energy savings. Highly recommended for any pool being converted from fixed-speed to VSP.

**Option 3 — Rough calculation.** If neither of the above is available, calculate by turnover: run the pump at a candidate RPM for a full turnover window and check the water clarity + skimmer performance. Iterate. This is slow but works.

**Head-pressure sanity check.** Whatever flow you're getting, the filter pressure gauge should read within the plumbing's design range. A pool designed for 40 GPM and a filter rated to 60 GPM should show a clean-filter pressure in the low double-digits (10–15 psi typical). If pressure is above 25 psi at your programmed flow, you're either past filter capacity or there's a downstream restriction — investigate before locking in the schedule.

**Flow vs. pressure — not the same thing.** Techs sometimes program to "hit a pressure number." That's wrong. Program to flow; pressure is what the plumbing produces at that flow. If flow is right and pressure looks wrong, the plumbing has changed (dirty filter, closed valve, scale in salt cell, etc.).
