# Module E — VSP Programming · Chapter 1: Why variable-speed pumps exist

*Original in-house authorship. Pentair and Hayward referenced by name + external link only. Consult the OEM manual for the exact keystrokes on any specific model — this course teaches concepts, not menus.*

A variable-speed pump (VSP) uses a permanent-magnet motor with an inverter drive to run the impeller at any RPM within the motor's rated range, instead of the fixed-speed on/off cycles of a legacy induction motor. The point is not "quieter" — the point is **energy consumption scales with the cube of speed** (see Chapter 2). Cutting the RPM in half cuts the electrical draw to roughly one-eighth. That efficiency arithmetic is why utilities and municipalities push VSPs, and why some U.S. states require them on new residential builds.

Two dominant residential brands in North America: **Pentair** (see pentair.com for their VSP product line and manuals) and **Hayward** (see hayward-pool.com). Both use the same physical principles; both expose those principles through slightly different on-pump keypads. This module teaches the **physics and the programming philosophy** so that a tech landing on any brand — or reading any OEM manual for a keystroke — knows *what* they're programming. It intentionally does not repeat any manufacturer's menu paths.
